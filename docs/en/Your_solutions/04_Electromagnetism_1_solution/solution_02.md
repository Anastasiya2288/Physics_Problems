# 2. Electric Potential — Detailed Solution

## 🔹 Key Definitions and Formulas

### 1. Electric Potential of a Point Charge

$$
V = k \frac{q}{r}
$$

Where:
- $V$ — electric potential  
- $k$ = 9 \times 10^9 \ \text{N·m}^2/\text{C}^2$ — Coulomb constant  
- $q$ — charge  
- $r$ — distance from the charge  

---

### 2. Principle of Superposition for Potential

Electric potential is a **scalar**, so we simply add values:

$$
V_{\text{total}} = \sum V_i
$$

---

### 3. Geometry of the Square

For a square with side $a = 1$ m:

$$
r = \frac{a}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
$$

(All charges are at the same distance from the center)

---

## 🔹 Step-by-Step Solution

### Step 1: Given Data

Charges at corners:
- $q_1 = +1$ C  
- $q_2 = -2$ C  
- $q_3 = +3$ C  
- $q_4 = -4$ C  

Distance from center:

$$
r = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
$$

---

### Step 2: Potential from Each Charge

Using:

$$
V = k \frac{q}{r}
$$

Since $r$ is the same for all charges:

$$
V_{\text{total}} = k \frac{1}{r} \left(q_1 + q_2 + q_3 + q_4\right)
$$

---

### Step 3: Sum of Charges

$$
q_{\text{sum}} = 1 - 2 + 3 - 4 = -2
$$

---

### Step 4: Substitute Values

$$
V_{\text{total}} = 9 \times 10^9 \cdot \frac{-2}{1/\sqrt{2}}
$$

---

### Step 5: Simplify

$$
\frac{1}{1/\sqrt{2}} = \sqrt{2}
$$

$$
V_{\text{total}} = 9 \times 10^9 \cdot (-2\sqrt{2})
$$

$$
V_{\text{total}} = -18\sqrt{2} \times 10^9
$$

---

### Step 6: Numerical Value

$$
\sqrt{2} \approx 1.414
$$

$$
V_{\text{total}} \approx -18 \times 1.414 \times 10^9
$$

$$
V_{\text{total}} \approx -2.55 \times 10^{10} \ \text{V}
$$

---

## 🔹 Final Answer

- Electric potential at the center:

$$
V \approx -2.55 \times 10^{10} \ \text{V}
$$

---

## 🔹 Key Insight

- Potential is **scalar**, so no vector cancellation like in force  
- Signs matter → negative charges dominate here  
- Even symmetric setups do NOT guarantee zero potential
