## 5. Field Levitation

### Useful Definitions and Formulas

- **Electric force** on a charge in an electric field:
$$F_E = eE$$
where $e$ is the charge and $E$ is the electric field strength (V/m).

- **Gravitational force** (weight):
$$F_g = m_p g$$

- **Condition for levitation** (equilibrium):
$$F_E = F_g$$

---

### Step-by-Step Solution

**Step 1: Write the equilibrium condition.**

For the proton to levitate, the upward electric force must exactly balance the downward gravitational force:

$$eE = m_p g$$

**Step 2: Solve for the electric field strength $E$.**

$$E = \frac{m_p g}{e}$$

**Step 3: Substitute the known values.**

$$E = \frac{(1.67 \times 10^{-27}\ \text{kg}) \times (9.8\ \text{m/s}^2)}{1.6 \times 10^{-19}\ \text{C}}$$

**Step 4: Calculate the numerator.**

$$m_p g = 1.67 \times 10^{-27} \times 9.8 = 1.6366 \times 10^{-26}\ \text{N}$$

**Step 5: Divide by the charge.**

$$E = \frac{1.6366 \times 10^{-26}}{1.6 \times 10^{-19}} \approx 1.02 \times 10^{-7}\ \text{V/m}$$

---

### Result

$$\boxed{E \approx 1.02 \times 10^{-7}\ \text{V/m}}$$

> **Comment:** This is an extraordinarily weak electric field — far smaller than fields we encounter in everyday electronics (typically $10^3$–$10^6$ V/m). This reflects the fact that the electromagnetic force is vastly stronger than gravity: even a tiny field is more than enough to support a proton against Earth's gravitational pull.
