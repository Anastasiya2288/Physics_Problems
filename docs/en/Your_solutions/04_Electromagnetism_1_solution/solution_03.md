# 3. Electrostatic Equilibrium — Detailed Solution

## 🔹 Key Definitions and Formulas

### 1. Coulomb’s Law

$$
F = k \frac{|q_1 q_2|}{r^2}
$$

Where:
- $F$ — electric force  
- $k$ = 9 \times 10^9 \ \text{N·m}^2/\text{C}^2$  
- $q_1, q_2$ — charges  
- $r$ — distance  

---

### 2. Condition for Equilibrium

A charge is in equilibrium when:

$$
\vec{F}_{\text{total}} = 0
$$

This means:
- Forces must be **equal in magnitude**
- Forces must act in **opposite directions**

---

## 🔹 Step-by-Step Solution

### Step 1: System Setup

- $q_1 = +4$ C  
- $q_2 = +9$ C  
- Distance between them: $2$ m  
- $q_3 = +1$ C (test charge)

👉 Since all charges are **positive**, forces on $q_3$ are **repulsive**.

So:
- $q_1$ pushes $q_3$ away  
- $q_2$ pushes $q_3$ away  

✅ Equilibrium is only possible **between the charges**

---

### Step 2: Define Position

Let:
- Distance from $q_1$ to $q_3$ = $x$  
- Distance from $q_2$ to $q_3$ = $2 - x$

---

### Step 3: Write Force Expressions

Force from $q_1$:

$$
F_1 = k \frac{q_1 q_3}{x^2}
$$

Force from $q_2$:

$$
F_2 = k \frac{q_2 q_3}{(2 - x)^2}
$$

---

### Step 4: Apply Equilibrium Condition

$$
F_1 = F_2
$$

$$
k \frac{4 \cdot 1}{x^2} = k \frac{9 \cdot 1}{(2 - x)^2}
$$

Cancel $k$:

$$
\frac{4}{x^2} = \frac{9}{(2 - x)^2}
$$

---

### Step 5: Solve the Equation

Take square root:

$$
\frac{2}{x} = \frac{3}{2 - x}
$$

Cross multiply:

$$
2(2 - x) = 3x
$$

$$
4 - 2x = 3x
$$

$$
4 = 5x
$$

$$
x = 0.8
$$

---

### Step 6: Interpret Result

- Distance from $q_1$ = $0.8$ m  
- Distance from $q_2$ = $2 - 0.8 = 1.2$ m  

---

## 🔹 Final Answer

- The equilibrium position is:

$$
x = 0.8 \ \text{m from } q_1
$$

or

$$
1.2 \ \text{m from } q_2
$$

---

## 🔹 Key Insight

- The point is **closer to the smaller charge**  
- This balances the stronger force from the larger charge  

👉 Bigger charge → must be farther away  
👉 Smaller charge → equilibrium point is closer
