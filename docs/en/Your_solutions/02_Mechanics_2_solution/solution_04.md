# 📘 Energy & Momentum — Inelastic Collision

---

## 🔑 Key Definitions and Formulas

### 📌 Conservation of Mechanical Energy (before collision)

For motion on a frictionless track:

$$
mgh=\frac{1}{2}mv^2
$$

This allows us to find the velocity just before impact.

---

### 📌 Conservation of Momentum (collision)

For an **inelastic collision** (objects stick together):

$$
m_1v_1+m_2v_2=\left(m_1+m_2\right)v_f
$$

where:
- $v_f$ — final velocity after collision

---

## 🧩 Given:

- $m_1=0.5\ \text{kg}$
- $m_2=1.5\ \text{kg}$
- $h=3.0\ \text{m}$
- $g=9.81\ \text{m/s}^2$
- $v_2=0$ (second block at rest)

---

# 🔍 Step 1: Find velocity before collision

Using energy conservation:

$$
mgh=\frac{1}{2}mv^2
$$

Mass cancels:

$$
gh=\frac{1}{2}v^2
$$

Multiply by 2:

$$
2gh=v^2
$$

$$
v=\sqrt{2gh}
$$

---

### 🔄 Substitute values:

$$
v=\sqrt{2\cdot9.81\cdot3.0}
$$

$$
v=\sqrt{58.86}
$$

$$
v\approx7.67\ \text{m/s}
$$

---

# 🔍 Step 2: Apply momentum conservation

Before collision:

$$
m_1v_1+m_2v_2=0.5\cdot7.67+1.5\cdot0
$$

$$
=3.835
$$

---

After collision:

$$
\left(m_1+m_2\right)v_f=2.0\cdot v_f
$$

---

# 🔍 Step 3: Solve for $v_f$

$$
3.835=2.0\cdot v_f
$$

$$
v_f=\frac{3.835}{2.0}
$$

$$
v_f\approx1.92\ \text{m/s}
$$

---

## ✅ Final Answer:

$$
v_f\approx1.92\ \text{m/s}
$$

---

# 🎯 Conclusion

- First, gravitational potential energy converts into kinetic energy.
- Then momentum is conserved during the collision.
- Because the collision is inelastic, **some energy is lost**, but momentum remains conserved.
