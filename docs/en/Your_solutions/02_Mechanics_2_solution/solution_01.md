#  Gravitational Dependence of a Simple Pendulum

---

## 🔑 Key Definitions and Formulas

A **simple pendulum** consists of a point mass suspended by a light, inextensible string, oscillating under the influence of gravity.

### 📌 Period of a Simple Pendulum

The period $T$ (time for one full oscillation) is given by:

$$
T=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}
$$

where:
- $T$ — period of oscillation
- $L$ — length of the pendulum
- $g$ — gravitational acceleration

---

## 🧠 Important Insight

The period depends on:
- **Length $L$** (longer → slower oscillation)
- **Gravity $g$** (weaker gravity → slower oscillation)

---

# 🧩 Part 1: Period on the Moon

###  Given:
- $T_E=4\ \text{s}$ (on Earth)
- $g_M=\frac{g_E}{6}$

---

### Step 1: Write ratio of periods

From the formula:

$$
T\propto\frac{1}{\sqrt{g}}
$$

So:

$$
\frac{T_M}{T_E}=\sqrt{\frac{g_E}{g_M}}
$$

---

###  Step 2: Substitute values

$$
\frac{T_M}{4}=\sqrt{\frac{g_E}{g_E/6}}=\sqrt{6}
$$

---

###  Step 3: Solve

$$
T_M=4\sqrt{6}
$$

---

### ✅ Final Answer:

$$
T_M\approx4\times2.45\approx9.8\ \text{s}
$$

---

# 🧩 Part 2: Length for $T=1$ second on Earth

###  Given:
- $T=1\ \text{s}$
- $g=9.81\ \text{m/s}^2$

---

###  Step 1: Start from formula

$$
T=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}
$$

---

###  Step 2: Solve for $L$

Divide both sides by $2\pi$:

$$
\frac{T}{2\pi}=\sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}
$$

Square both sides:

$$
\frac{T^2}{4\pi^2}=\frac{L}{g}
$$

Multiply by $g$:

$$
L=\frac{gT^2}{4\pi^2}
$$

---

###  Step 3: Substitute values

$$
L=\frac{9.81\cdot1^2}{4\pi^2}
$$

$$
L=\frac{9.81}{39.48}
$$

---

###  Step 4: Calculate

$$
L\approx0.248\ \text{m}
$$

---

### ✅ Final Answer:

$$
L\approx0.25\ \text{m}
$$

---

# 🎯 Final Results

- Period on Moon:  
  $$T_M\approx9.8\ \text{s}$$

- Required length for $1$ second pendulum:  
  $$L\approx0.25\ \text{m}$$
