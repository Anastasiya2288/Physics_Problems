
# Energy Stored by Charge in a Capacitor

## Useful Definitions and Formulas

### 1. Capacitance of a Parallel-Plate Capacitor

The capacitance describes the ability of a capacitor to store electric charge.

For a parallel-plate capacitor:

C=\varepsilon_0\frac{S}{d}

where:

- $C$ — capacitance
- $\varepsilon_0=8.85\times10^{-12}\ \mathrm{F/m}$ — permittivity of free space
- $S$ — plate area
- $d$ — distance between plates

---

### 2. Energy Stored in a Capacitor

The energy accumulated in the electric field of a capacitor is:

W=\frac12CU^2

where:

- $W$ — stored energy
- $C$ — capacitance
- $U$ — voltage

---

### 3. Electric Field Intensity

The electric field between two parallel plates is:

E=\frac{U}{d}

where:

- $E$ — electric field intensity
- $U$ — voltage
- $d$ — separation between plates

---

### 4. Force Between Capacitor Plates

The attractive force between the plates can be calculated using:

F=\frac12\varepsilon_0ES

However, using the electric field energy density relation, the standard form becomes:

F=\frac12\varepsilon_0E^2S

where:

- $F$ — force of attraction
- $E$ — electric field intensity
- $S$ — plate area

---

# Step-by-Step Solution

## Step 1. Write the Given Information

Plate area:

$$
S=0.02\ \mathrm{m^2}
$$

Distance between plates:

$$
d=5\ \mathrm{mm}=0.005\ \mathrm{m}
$$

Voltage:

$$
U=500\ \mathrm{V}
$$

Permittivity of free space:

$$
\varepsilon_0=8.85\times10^{-12}\ \mathrm{F/m}
$$

---

# Part 1. Calculate the Capacitance

Use the formula:

$$
C=\varepsilon_0\frac{S}{d}
$$

Substitute the values:

$$
C=8.85\times10^{-12}\times\frac{0.02}{0.005}
$$

First calculate the fraction:

$$
\frac{0.02}{0.005}=4
$$

Then:

$$
C=8.85\times10^{-12}\times4
$$

$$
C=3.54\times10^{-11}\ \mathrm{F}
$$

---

# Part 2. Calculate the Stored Energy

Use:

$$
W=\frac12CU^2
$$

Substitute the values:

$$
W=\frac12\times3.54\times10^{-11}\times500^2
$$

Calculate:

$$
500^2=250000
$$

Then:

$$
W=0.5\times3.54\times10^{-11}\times250000
$$

$$
W=4.43\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm{J}
$$

---

# Part 3. Calculate the Electric Field Intensity

Use:

$$
E=\frac{U}{d}
$$

Substitute the values:

$$
E=\frac{500}{0.005}
$$

$$
E=100000\ \mathrm{V/m}
$$

or:

$$
E=1.0\times10^5\ \mathrm{V/m}
$$

---

# Part 4. Calculate the Force of Attraction

Use:

$$
F=\frac12\varepsilon_0E^2S
$$

Substitute the values:

$$
F=\frac12\times8.85\times10^{-12}\times\left(1.0\times10^5\right)^2\times0.02
$$

Calculate the square:

$$
\left(1.0\times10^5\right)^2=1.0\times10^{10}
$$

Then:

$$
F=0.5\times8.85\times10^{-12}\times1.0\times10^{10}\times0.02
$$

$$
F=8.85\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{N}
$$

---

# Final Answers

## 1. Capacitance

$$
\boxed{
C=3.54\times10^{-11}\ \mathrm{F}
}
$$

---

## 2. Stored Energy

$$
\boxed{
W=4.43\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm{J}
}
$$

---

## 3. Electric Field Intensity

$$
\boxed{
E=1.0\times10^5\ \mathrm{V/m}
}
$$

---

## 4. Force Between the Plates

$$
\boxed{
F=8.85\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{N}
}
$$

---

# Physical Interpretation

- The capacitor stores electric energy in the electric field created between the plates.
- A larger plate area increases capacitance because more charge can be stored.
- A smaller distance between plates increases both capacitance and electric field intensity.
- Opposite charges on the plates attract each other, creating the calculated force.
