# Magnetic Field Between Two Parallel Wires (Ampere’s Law Context)

## 🔹 Key Definitions and Formulas

### 1. Magnetic Field of a Long Straight Wire

The magnetic field at a distance $r$ from a long straight current-carrying wire is:

$$
B=\frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r}
$$

where:
- $B$ — magnetic field (T)
- $\mu_0=4\pi\times10^{-7}T\cdot m/A$ — permeability of free space
- $I$ — current (A)
- $r$ — distance from the wire (m)

---

### 2. Superposition Principle

If multiple magnetic fields are present:

$$
B_{total}=B_1+B_2
$$

Magnetic fields **add vectorially**.

---

### 3. Right-Hand Rule

- Thumb → direction of current
- Fingers → direction of magnetic field lines (circular around wire)

---

## 🔹 Step-by-Step Solution

### Step 1: Convert given values

- Distance between wires: $d=10cm=0.10m$
- Midpoint distance from each wire:

$$
r=\frac{d}{2}=0.05m
$$

- Current: $I=5A$

---

### Step 2: Magnetic field from one wire

$$
B=\frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r}
$$

Substitute values:

$$
B=\frac{4\pi\times10^{-7}\times5}{2\pi\times0.05}
$$

Simplify:

$$
B=\frac{20\pi\times10^{-7}}{0.1\pi}
$$

$$
B=200\times10^{-7}=2\times10^{-5}T
$$

---

### Step 3: Direction of fields

- The currents are in **opposite directions**
- Using the **right-hand rule**, the magnetic fields at the midpoint:

👉 **point in the same direction**

So they **add up**

---

### Step 4: Total magnetic field

$$
B_{total}=B+B=2\times2\times10^{-5}
$$

$$
B_{total}=4\times10^{-5}T
$$

---

## ✅ Final Answer

$$
B=4\times10^{-5}T
$$

**Direction:** perpendicular to the plane of the wires (same direction from both wires, determined by the right-hand rule)

---

## 🔹 Interpretation

Because the currents flow in opposite directions, the magnetic fields between the wires reinforce each other, leading to a stronger net magnetic field at the midpoint.
