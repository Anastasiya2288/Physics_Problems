
# Conservation of Energy — Pendulum Problem

## Useful Definitions and Formulas

### 1. Conservation of Mechanical Energy

The total mechanical energy of a system remains constant if no non-conservative forces such as friction or air resistance act on it.

For a pendulum:

- At the highest point:
  - energy is mostly gravitational potential energy
  - speed is zero

- At the lowest point:
  - potential energy becomes minimum
  - kinetic energy becomes maximum

The conservation law is:

E_i=E_f

or more specifically:

$$
mgh=\frac12mv^2
$$

where:

- $m$ — mass of the pendulum bob
- $g$ — gravitational acceleration
- $h$ — vertical height difference
- $v$ — speed at the bottom

---

### 2. Height of a Pendulum

For a pendulum released from an angle $\theta$:

h=L\left(1-\cos\theta\right)

where:

- $L$ — pendulum length
- $\theta$ — initial angle

---

### 3. Final Speed Formula

Combining the formulas above:

$$
mgh=\frac12mv^2
$$

The masses cancel:

$$
gh=\frac12v^2
$$

Solving for velocity:

v=\sqrt{2gh}

---

# Step-by-Step Solution

## Step 1. Write the Given Information

Pendulum length:

$$
L=1.0\ \mathrm{m}
$$

Initial angle:

$$
\theta=15^\circ
$$

Gravitational acceleration:

$$
g=9.81\ \mathrm{m/s^2}
$$

---

# Step 2. Calculate the Vertical Height

Use the pendulum height formula:

$$
h=L\left(1-\cos\theta\right)
$$

Substitute the values:

$$
h=1.0\left(1-\cos15^\circ\right)
$$

Since:

$$
\cos15^\circ\approx0.9659
$$

then:

$$
h=1.0\left(1-0.9659\right)
$$

$$
h=0.0341\ \mathrm{m}
$$

---

# Step 3. Apply Conservation of Energy

Use:

$$
v=\sqrt{2gh}
$$

Substitute the known values:

$$
v=\sqrt{2\times9.81\times0.0341}
$$

---

# Step 4. Perform the Calculation

First calculate inside the square root:

$$
2\times9.81\times0.0341\approx0.669
$$

Now take the square root:

$$
v\approx0.82\ \mathrm{m/s}
$$

---

# Final Answer

$$
\boxed{
v\approx0.82\ \mathrm{m/s}
}
$$

---

# Explanation of the Result

- The pendulum starts with gravitational potential energy.
- As it swings downward, this energy transforms into kinetic energy.
- At the bottom of the swing, the pendulum has its maximum speed.
- Because the starting angle is relatively small ($15^\circ$), the height difference is also small, resulting in a moderate speed.
